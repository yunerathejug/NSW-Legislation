Coroners Amendment (Senior Next of Kin)

Explanatory note
This explanatory note relates to this Bill as introduced into Parliament.

Overview of Bill
The object of this Bill is to implement recommendations arising from the statutory review of the
Coroners Act 2009 (the Act) relating to—
(a) determining the person who is, or is presumed to be, the senior next of kin of a deceased
      person for the Act and enabling coroners to appoint a person as the senior next of kin if the
      presumed senior next of kin is not available or is not an appropriate person to act as the
      senior next of kin, and
(b) recognising extended family or kin structures in different cultures, including in Aboriginal
      and Torres Strait Islander cultures, and
(c) enabling the senior next of kin to exercise functions verbally, and
(d) making and applying for orders in relation to the release of a deceased person’s remains.

Outline of provisions
Clause 1 sets out the name, also called the short title, of the proposed Act.
Clause 2 provides for the commencement of the proposed Act.

Schedule 1[5] amends the definition of relative.
Schedule 1[6]—
(a) substitutes section 6A to provide for the person who is considered to be the senior next of

(d) provides in proposed section 6D for applications to the Supreme Court for an order to vary
     a coroner’s appointment of the next of kin of a deceased person.
Schedule 1[7]–[9] make consequential amendments.
Schedule 1[10] provides for applications to the Supreme Court for an order to revoke a decision
by a coroner to issue an exhumation warrant under the Act, section 91.
Schedule 1[12] substitutes the Act, section 96 to update provisions about objections by the senior
next of kin of a deceased person to the carrying out of post mortem investigations by a coroner.
Schedule 1[13] makes a consequential amendment.
Schedule 1[14] substitutes the Act, section 98 to update provisions about the senior next of kin of
a deceased person authorising another person to carry out a function of the senior next of kin.
Schedule 1[11] makes a consequential amendment.
Schedule 1[2], [15], [17]–[24] update language in the Act to remove references to the disposal of
human remains.
Schedule 1[25]—
(a) substitutes the Act, section 101 to provide for orders made by a coroner for—
     (i)    the burial, cremation or other interment of human remains, or
     (ii) the delivery or handing over of human remains for anatomical or medical research, or
     (iii) the removal of human remains from the State, and
(b) provides in proposed section 101AA for an application to the Supreme Court for an order
     to vary—
     (i)    the person to whom an order under the Act, section 101 is given, or
     (ii) the conditions of an order under the Act, section 101.
Schedule 1[16] makes an amendment consequent on Schedule 1[25].
